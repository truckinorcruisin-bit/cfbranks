    How the points work

    Recursive (default). A win over an FBS team earns a flat base credit plus a bonus tied to that team’s own score. That score is built the same way, from the wins it earned over other teams, and so on. The calculation repeats until the numbers stop changing, so a 5-2 record built on weak opponents ends up worth much less than a 5-2 record built on strong ones. An average opponent is worth 1.0 per win. The quality weight sets how much of each win comes from the opponent’s score instead of the flat credit.

    Opponent wins + schedule. A win is worth the beaten team’s FBS win total, multiplied by the average FBS win percentage of its opponents relative to the national average. The schedule weight scales that multiplier toward 1.00 (half) or removes it (off).

    Elo. Every team starts at 1500 and trades rating points after each FBS game, in date order. Before kickoff, the rating gap sets a win probability (a 400-point gap is about 10-to-1, and the home team gets a 55-point boost). The winner then takes 32 times the chance it had of losing: an upset moves ratings a lot, an expected win barely moves them. The margin of victory option scales each swing by the size of the win (capped at 35 points, and damped when a favorite wins big). There are no preseason ratings, so this is a pure in-season rating. The bar shows rating points gained from wins.

    My rank. Uses opponents’ current AP rank (the latest poll in the data), not their rank when played. Wins: +2 over a top-15 team. Otherwise +1 if the opponent has two or more wins over teams with winning records (“quality wins”), or +0.5 if the opponent has a winning record but fewer than two. Close wins: a win by 8 or fewer over a team outside the top 40 costs 0.5, on top of any win credit. Losses: +0.5 for losing by 8 or fewer to a top-10 team, −1 for losing to a team ranked 21 to 40, and −1.5 for losing to a team outside the top 40. Other losses score nothing. A winning record means above .500 in FBS games. The AP poll names only 25 teams. Teams receiving votes, when the data includes them, rank next by vote total, and the remaining spots through 40 are filled in by Elo rating. The menu can limit quality wins to opponents from the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC or Notre Dame. The bar shows points earned before deductions.

    Games against FCS opponents are removed everywhere. A team that is 4-3 with one FCS win counts as 3-3 here, and a win over an FCS team earns nothing. Losses add nothing directly.

    The Movers tab replays the season one week at a time, scoring each system with only the games played so far (and, for My rank, the AP poll released after that week), then shows who rose and fell the most.

    The Spreads tab turns each system’s pre-week ratings into a point spread for this week’s games and scores how far it sits from the sportsbook line.

    The Performance tab grades each system by betting $5 a week on its 10 biggest disagreements with Vegas at −110 and tracking the running return.
